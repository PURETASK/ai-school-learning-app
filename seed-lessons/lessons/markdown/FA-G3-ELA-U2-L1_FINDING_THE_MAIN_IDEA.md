## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will distinguish topic from main idea and support a main idea with key details from a short informational text.
- **Essential Question:** How do readers figure out what a text is mostly teaching?
- **Development Notes:** Third graders often confuse topic with main idea. Use short passages and evidence sorting before asking for written summaries.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **topic:** who or what the text is mostly about
- **main idea:** the most important point the author wants you to understand
- **key detail:** an important fact that supports the main idea
- **evidence:** proof from the text
- **summary:** a short retelling of the most important ideas

### Prerequisites
- Read a grade-level informational paragraph
- Identify important facts
- Retell a short passage in order


## 5. Student Success Criteria

- I can explain the core concept in my own words.

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

Students see three details: “They have roots,” “They need sunlight,” “They grow from seeds.” They decide what these details are mostly about and what main idea they support.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will distinguish topic from main idea and support a main idea with key details from a short informational text.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

The topic is the subject. The main idea is the important message about that subject. Details are pieces of evidence that prove the main idea. If the topic is plants, the main idea might be “Plants need several things to grow.”


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Passage: “Bees visit flowers to collect nectar. As bees move, pollen sticks to their bodies. When they land on another flower, the pollen can help the plant make seeds.” Topic: bees and flowers. Main idea: Bees help many plants make seeds. Key details: bees collect nectar, pollen sticks, pollen moves to another flower.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** Two students choose different main ideas. One matches all three key details; one matches only the first detail. Which is stronger and why?

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: The paragraph is mostly about how bees help plants. Choose two details from the text that prove the claim and explain how they support it.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret the author’s purpose: Is the author trying to entertain, explain, or persuade? What clues show that?

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students highlight details in a paragraph, drag them into “supports main idea” or “extra detail,” then write one main idea sentence.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** Main idea is built from details.

### Smallest Parts
- topic
- important details
- repeated ideas
- author message
- evidence from the text

### Known Facts
- Texts include many details.
- Some details are more important than others.
- A main idea must match most of the important details.

### Unknowns
- The author’s most important point.

### Rules / Patterns
- A topic is usually a word or phrase.
- A main idea is usually a full sentence.
- Key details must support the main idea.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not choose a tiny detail as the main idea.
- Do not choose an idea that only matches one sentence.
- Do not confuse “plants” with “plants need water, sunlight, and soil to grow.”

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Identify the topic.
- Underline repeated or important details.
- Ask: What do these details prove together?
- Write a main idea sentence.
- Check that each key detail supports it.

### Answer Check
- If a detail does not support the main idea, revise the main idea or choose stronger evidence.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Sort cards into topic, main idea, and detail.
- Choose the best main idea for a paragraph about frogs.
- Cross out one detail that is interesting but not key.
- Explain why “bees” is a topic but not a complete main idea.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which is a topic, not a main idea? Choices: Butterflies; Butterflies change during their life cycle.; Butterflies begin as eggs.; Butterflies use wings to fly.
- **Correct Answer:** Butterflies
- **Explanation:** A topic is the subject, not the full important point.
- **Skill Tag:** ELA.TOPIC_VS_MAIN_IDEA

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q02 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which main idea matches details about roots, stems, leaves, and flowers? Choices: Plants have parts that help them live.; Flowers are colorful.; Roots are underground.; Leaves can be green.
- **Correct Answer:** Plants have parts that help them live.
- **Explanation:** It covers all the key details.
- **Skill Tag:** ELA.MAIN_IDEA

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q03 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Write a main idea for details about exercise helping your heart, muscles, and mood.
- **Correct Answer:** Exercise helps the body in many ways.
- **Explanation:** The answer should cover all listed details.
- **Skill Tag:** ELA.SUMMARY

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q04 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which detail best supports “Dogs can help people”? Choices: Dogs have fur.; Some dogs guide people who cannot see.; Dogs can be brown.; Dogs like naps.
- **Correct Answer:** Some dogs guide people who cannot see.
- **Explanation:** It directly proves dogs can help.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.RELEVANCE

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q05 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student says the main idea of a paragraph about animal habitats is “trees.” What might be wrong?
- **Correct Answer:** “Trees” is probably only a topic or detail, not a complete main idea about habitats.
- **Explanation:** Main ideas are complete points.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.TOPIC_AS_MAIN_IDEA

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q06 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** A main idea should match... Choices: only the first sentence; most important details; the longest word; one fun fact
- **Correct Answer:** most important details
- **Explanation:** Main ideas are supported by key details.
- **Skill Tag:** ELA.MAIN_IDEA_CHECK

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q07 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why is evidence important when choosing a main idea?
- **Correct Answer:** Evidence proves the main idea is supported by the text.
- **Explanation:** Students should name proof/details.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.REASONING

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q08 [apply | medium]
- **Question:** Read this detail set: “Clouds darken. Wind grows stronger. Rain begins to fall.” Write the main idea.
- **Correct Answer:** A storm is beginning, or weather changes before rain.
- **Explanation:** Accept a sentence covering all details.
- **Skill Tag:** INTERPRET.DETAILS

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q09 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which detail is least useful for a paragraph about how libraries help readers? Choices: Libraries have books.; Libraries let people borrow books.; Some libraries have blue chairs.; Librarians help people find books.
- **Correct Answer:** Some libraries have blue chairs.
- **Explanation:** It is not central to how libraries help readers.
- **Skill Tag:** CRITICAL.RELEVANCE

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q10 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Turn the topic “volcanoes” into a main idea sentence.
- **Correct Answer:** Example: Volcanoes can change the land when they erupt.
- **Explanation:** A complete sentence about volcanoes is required.
- **Skill Tag:** CREATE.MAIN_IDEA

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q11 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** What should you do before writing the main idea? Choices: Guess quickly; Find key details; Count the letters; Skip the passage
- **Correct Answer:** Find key details
- **Explanation:** Details are the evidence.
- **Skill Tag:** PROCESS.READING

### FA-G3-ELA-U2-L1-Q12 [explain | hard]
- **Question:** How can two details work together to prove one main idea?
- **Correct Answer:** They show related evidence that points to the same important message.
- **Explanation:** Look for connection language.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.SYNTHESIS

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student confuses a topic or interesting detail with the main idea.
- **Simple Explanation:** A topic names the subject. A main idea says the big point about that subject.

### Reteach Steps
1. Ask what the paragraph is mostly about.
2. Circle three important details.
3. Ask what the details have in common.
4. Write a full-sentence main idea.
5. Check each detail against the sentence.

### Reteach Practice

- Topic or main idea: “sharks” → **Topic**
- Topic or main idea: “Sharks have special body parts that help them hunt.” → **Main idea**
- Choose a key detail for “Plants need care.” → **Plants need water.**
- Fix: Main idea = “sunlight” for a plant-growth passage → **Plants need sunlight, water, and soil to grow.**
- What proves a main idea? → **Key details/evidence**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.

## 14. Challenge Path
- Compare two possible main ideas and rank which one is more precise.
- Write a 3-sentence summary using one main idea and two key details.
- Find a detail that does not belong and explain why it weakens the summary.
