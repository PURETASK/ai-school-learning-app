# SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1 — Planning, Monitoring, and Evaluating Learning

**Academy:** Scholar Academy  
**Grade:** 9  
**Subject/Course:** Study Skills / Grade 9 Study Skills  
**Unit:** Unit 1: Learning How to Learn  
**Estimated Time:** 30 minutes

## Learning Objective
Students will use metacognition to plan, monitor, and evaluate a learning task.

## Essential Question
How do strong learners know what to do before, during, and after learning?

## Standards Tags
- SEL.G9.SELF_MANAGEMENT.METACOGNITION
- COLLEGE_READY.G9.STUDY_SKILLS.PLANNING

## Thinking Skill Tags
- metacognition
- learning planner
- evidence-based reasoning
- retrieval practice

## Vocabulary
- **metacognition**: Thinking about your thinking.
- **monitor**: To check progress while working.
- **evaluate**: To judge what worked after finishing.
- **strategy**: A planned way to approach a task.

## Universal Lesson Flow

### Hook
A student studies for two hours but still fails the quiz because they reread notes without checking understanding.

**Student Action:** Diagnose what went wrong in the study plan.

**Success Criteria:**
- Identifies weak monitoring or passive study.

### Learning Goal
I can plan, monitor, and evaluate my learning so I know what to do next.

**Student Action:** Define plan, monitor, evaluate.

**Success Criteria:**
- Explains each metacognitive step.

### Mini Teach
Metacognition means thinking about your thinking. Strong learners plan before work, monitor during work, and evaluate after work.

**Student Action:** Match each phase to a student action.

**Success Criteria:**
- Matches plan/monitor/evaluate correctly.

### First-Principles Breakdown
Break learning down: What is the task? What do I already know? What strategy will I use? How will I know if it worked?

**Student Action:** Complete a planning checklist.

**Success Criteria:**
- Builds a specific learning plan.

### Worked Example
Before biology review: I need cell organelles. I know nucleus and membrane. I will use retrieval cards. I will test myself without looking.

**Student Action:** Identify plan, monitor, evaluate in the example.

**Success Criteria:**
- Labels the three metacognitive phases.

### Guided Practice
Create a study plan for an upcoming algebra quiz.

**Student Action:** Choose goal, strategy, check point, and after-action review.

**Success Criteria:**
- Creates a realistic study plan.

### Critical Thinking Checkpoint
Which is stronger: rereading for 30 minutes or self-testing for 15 minutes? Defend your answer.

**Student Action:** Compare strategies using retention evidence.

**Success Criteria:**
- Explains why retrieval is stronger than passive rereading.

### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task
Claim: My strategy worked. Evidence: I answered 8/10 without notes. Reasoning: Accurate recall shows I can retrieve the information.

**Student Action:** Write claim/evidence/reasoning about study success.

**Success Criteria:**
- Uses evidence, not feelings alone.

### Interpretation / Discussion Task
Discussion frame: 'I used to think studying meant ___. Now I think it means ___ because ___.'

**Student Action:** Reflect on a change in understanding.

**Success Criteria:**
- Explains a learning shift.

### Active Practice
Use the Learning Planner to plan one real school task.

**Student Action:** Fill in task, first step, strategy, obstacle, finish signal.

**Success Criteria:**
- Completes all planning fields.

### Retrieval Check
Without looking, name the three metacognition moves.

**Student Action:** Recall plan, monitor, evaluate.

**Success Criteria:**
- Recalls all three.

### Feedback
Feedback checks whether the plan has a specific task, active strategy, monitoring check, and evaluation step.

**Student Action:** Revise a weak study plan.

**Success Criteria:**
- Adds a measurable check for understanding.

### Reflection
What strategy will you use the next time you feel stuck?

**Student Action:** Write a next-time learning rule.

**Success Criteria:**
- Names a transferable strategy.

## Quiz and Answer Key

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q1** (mc): What is metacognition?  
  - Correct/Expected: Thinking about your thinking  
  - Explanation: Metacognition helps students manage and improve learning.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q2** (mc): Which action is planning?  
  - Correct/Expected: Choosing a strategy before starting  
  - Explanation: Planning happens before the task begins.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q3** (mc): Which action is monitoring?  
  - Correct/Expected: Asking 'Do I understand this?' while working  
  - Explanation: Monitoring is checking progress during learning.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q4** (mc): Which action is evaluating?  
  - Correct/Expected: Deciding what worked after finishing  
  - Explanation: Evaluation happens after learning evidence appears.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q5** (mc): Which study strategy gives stronger evidence of learning?  
  - Correct/Expected: Answering from memory  
  - Explanation: Retrieval practice shows what the student can remember.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q6** (mc): What should a good learning plan include?  
  - Correct/Expected: Task, strategy, checkpoint, finish signal  
  - Explanation: A useful plan tells the student what to do and how to check it.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q7** (short_answer): Write a plan-monitor-evaluate routine for studying vocabulary.  
  - Correct/Expected: Plan: choose 10 words and cover definitions. Monitor: check which words I miss. Evaluate: retest missed words tomorrow.  
  - Explanation: A strong routine includes before, during, and after actions.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q8** (explain): Explain why 'I studied a long time' is weaker evidence than 'I recalled 8/10 terms.'  
  - Correct/Expected: Time spent does not prove learning; recall accuracy shows what I can retrieve.  
  - Explanation: Learning evidence should measure understanding or recall.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q9** (cer): Use CER: Claim that self-testing helped you learn.  
  - Correct/Expected: Claim: Self-testing helped me learn. Evidence: I remembered more terms without notes. Reasoning: Remembering without notes shows retrieval strength.  
  - Explanation: This uses evidence to evaluate learning.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q10** (short_answer): Name one obstacle that can hurt a study plan and how to handle it.  
  - Correct/Expected: A phone can distract me, so I can put it away for 15 minutes while I self-test.  
  - Explanation: Good plans anticipate barriers.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q11** (explain): Explain one important idea from planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning, not just recognize it.

- **SA-G9-STUDY-U1-L1-Q12** (explain): Explain one important idea from planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning, not just recognize it.

## Reteach Path
Studying is not just time. Studying works when you can prove what you remember or understand.

- Name the task
- Choose active strategy
- Check understanding midway
- Retest without notes
- Decide next step

## Challenge Path
- Design a 7-day study plan using Memory Vault review days.
- Compare two study strategies and defend the stronger one with evidence.
- Create a personal Learning Planner template.
