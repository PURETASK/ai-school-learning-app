## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will interpret variables, write algebraic expressions from contexts, evaluate expressions, and explain the structure of expressions.
- **Essential Question:** How can symbols represent quantities we do not know yet or quantities that change?
- **Development Notes:** Ninth graders need algebra as structure and representation, not symbol manipulation only. Connect variables to changing quantities and constraints.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **variable:** a symbol that represents a number or quantity
- **expression:** a mathematical phrase without an equals sign
- **coefficient:** a number multiplied by a variable
- **constant:** a fixed number
- **evaluate:** to find the value of an expression for given variable values
- **term:** a part of an expression separated by addition or subtraction

### Prerequisites
- Use order of operations
- Understand arithmetic operations
- Read simple word problems

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

A streaming service charges $8 plus $3 per movie rental. Students decide what changes, what stays fixed, and how to represent total cost for any number of movies.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will interpret variables, write algebraic expressions from contexts, evaluate expressions, and explain the structure of expressions.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

A variable lets algebra describe many possible cases at once. In 8 + 3m, 8 is a constant starting cost, 3m means 3 dollars for each movie, and m represents the number of movies. The expression has no equals sign because it describes a quantity, not a full equation.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Context: $5 entry fee plus $2 per game. Let g = number of games. Expression: 5 + 2g. If g = 4, then 5 + 2(4) = 13 dollars.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** How can substituting m = 1 help test whether 8 + 3m represents the streaming-service context correctly?

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: 8 + 3m fits the context. Use the fixed fee and per-movie fee as evidence.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret each term in 12 + 0.5d for a taxi ride that charges $12 plus $0.50 per mile.

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students build expressions from context cards by dragging fixed costs, coefficients, variables, and operations into an expression builder.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** Algebraic expressions model relationships among quantities.

### Smallest Parts
- unknown or changing quantity
- operation
- coefficient
- constant
- terms
- context units
- structure

### Known Facts
- There is an $8 base charge.
- Each movie costs $3.
- The number of movies can change.

### Unknowns
- Total cost for any number of movies.

### Rules / Patterns
- Variables represent quantities, not labels.
- A coefficient multiplies the variable.
- Constants do not change.
- Evaluate by substituting a value for the variable and following order of operations.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not treat 3m as 3 + m.
- Do not ignore units or context.
- Do not put an equals sign unless comparing two quantities.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Identify the changing quantity.
- Assign a variable.
- Identify fixed and repeated amounts.
- Write terms for each part.
- Combine terms into an expression.
- Evaluate by substitution to test meaning.

### Answer Check
- Substitute a simple value, such as m = 0 or m = 1, and see if the expression matches the context.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Identify the variable in 7 + 4x and explain what it could represent.
- Write an expression for 10 plus 6 times a number n.
- Evaluate 2x + 5 when x = 3.
- Debug: A student writes 3 + m for $3 per movie.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which is an expression? Choices: 3x + 7; 3x + 7 = 19; x = 4; 7 < x
- **Correct Answer:** 3x + 7
- **Explanation:** An expression has no equals or inequality sign.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.EXPRESSION

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q02 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Write an expression for 9 more than twice a number n.
- **Correct Answer:** 2n + 9
- **Explanation:** Twice a number is 2n; 9 more adds 9.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.WRITE

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q03 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** In 5x + 12, what is the coefficient? Choices: 5; x; 12; 17
- **Correct Answer:** 5
- **Explanation:** The coefficient multiplies the variable.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.VOCAB

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q04 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Evaluate 3a - 4 when a = 6.
- **Correct Answer:** 14
- **Explanation:** 3(6) - 4 = 18 - 4 = 14.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.EVALUATE

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q05 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student interprets 4x as 4 + x. Fix the misconception.
- **Correct Answer:** 4x means 4 multiplied by x, or four groups of x.
- **Explanation:** Juxtaposition means multiplication.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.COEFFICIENT

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q06 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why does 8 + 3m have a constant and a variable term?
- **Correct Answer:** 8 is fixed; 3m changes based on the number of movies.
- **Explanation:** Connect structure to context.
- **Skill Tag:** STRUCTURE.REASONING

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q07 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which expression represents 5 dollars per ticket for t tickets? Choices: 5 + t; 5t; t - 5; 5/t
- **Correct Answer:** 5t
- **Explanation:** $5 for each ticket means 5 times t.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.CONTEXT

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q08 [apply | medium]
- **Question:** A gym charges $20 plus $15 per month. Write an expression for m months.
- **Correct Answer:** 20 + 15m
- **Explanation:** Fixed fee plus monthly fee.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.MODEL

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q09 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which value checks 20 + 15m for zero months? Choices: 20; 15; 35; 0
- **Correct Answer:** 20
- **Explanation:** If m=0, the monthly term is 0, leaving the fixed fee.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.CHECK

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q10 [short | easy]
- **Question:** Name the terms in 4x + 7.
- **Correct Answer:** 4x and 7
- **Explanation:** Terms are separated by addition/subtraction.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.STRUCTURE

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q11 [evidence | medium]
- **Question:** Prove that 2n + 5 represents “5 more than twice n.”
- **Correct Answer:** 2n means twice n; +5 means 5 more.
- **Explanation:** Requires structure explanation.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.EXPRESSION

### SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1-Q12 [challenge | hard]
- **Question:** Write two different contexts for 10 + 2x and explain what 10, 2, and x mean in each.
- **Correct Answer:** Two valid contexts with interpreted terms.
- **Explanation:** Transfer understanding.
- **Skill Tag:** ALG.TRANSFER

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student treats variable terms as addition or treats variables as labels instead of quantities.
- **Simple Explanation:** A variable stands for a quantity. A coefficient tells how many groups of that quantity.

### Reteach Steps
1. Underline changing quantity.
2. Choose a variable.
3. Circle fixed amounts.
4. Write repeated/per-unit quantities as coefficient-variable terms.
5. Substitute a value to check.

### Reteach Practice

- Expression or equation: 3x + 2 → **Expression**
- Coefficient in 7p → **7**
- Evaluate x + 6 when x=4 → **10**
- Write 3 times n → **3n**
- Fix: 4m = 4 + m → **4m = 4 × m**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.
