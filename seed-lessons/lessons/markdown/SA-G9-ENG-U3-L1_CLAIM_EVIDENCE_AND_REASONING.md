## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will construct a claim, select relevant evidence, and write reasoning that explains how the evidence supports the claim.
- **Essential Question:** What makes an academic answer convincing instead of just opinion?
- **Development Notes:** Ninth graders often quote evidence without explaining it. This lesson emphasizes the reasoning bridge between evidence and claim.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **claim:** a debatable statement or answer that needs support
- **evidence:** specific proof such as facts, quotes, examples, or data
- **reasoning:** the explanation of how evidence proves the claim
- **relevance:** how directly evidence connects to the claim
- **counterclaim:** an opposing or alternative claim

### Prerequisites
- Identify main idea or argument
- Quote or paraphrase a source
- Write complete analytical sentences

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

Students compare two answers: “The character is brave” and “The character is brave because she enters the cave to rescue her brother even though she is afraid, showing courage is action despite fear.” They decide which is stronger and why.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will construct a claim, select relevant evidence, and write reasoning that explains how the evidence supports the claim.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

Academic reasoning often follows Claim → Evidence → Reasoning. The claim states the answer. Evidence gives proof. Reasoning explains why the proof matters. Evidence without reasoning leaves the reader to guess the connection.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Claim: The narrator values independence. Evidence: She refuses help and says she wants to solve the problem herself. Reasoning: Her refusal shows she wants control over her choices, which supports the idea that independence matters to her.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** Which is stronger evidence: a direct action by the character or a vague description of the setting? Explain based on the claim.

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: The speaker is frustrated. Select the strongest quote and explain the language that proves frustration.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret how word choice changes the strength of evidence. Which words carry emotional meaning?

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students assemble CER blocks into a coherent analytical paragraph and revise weak reasoning.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** Strong arguments are built from claim, proof, and explanation.

### Smallest Parts
- claim
- evidence
- reasoning
- source/context
- relevance
- counterclaim
- audience

### Known Facts
- A claim needs support.
- Evidence must be specific.
- Reasoning connects evidence to the claim.

### Unknowns
- Which evidence is strongest and how it should be explained.

### Rules / Patterns
- Claims should be specific and arguable.
- Evidence should be accurate and relevant.
- Reasoning must explain the connection.
- Counterclaims can strengthen analysis when addressed fairly.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not use evidence that only sounds related.
- Do not drop a quote without explanation.
- Do not treat opinion as proof.
- Do not overclaim beyond what the evidence supports.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Write a precise claim.
- List possible evidence.
- Rank evidence by relevance and strength.
- Explain how the evidence proves the claim.
- Check for gaps or counterclaims.

### Answer Check
- If the reader could ask “so what?” after the evidence, add more reasoning.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Sort statements into claim, evidence, and reasoning.
- Choose the strongest evidence for a claim about perseverance.
- Add reasoning to a quote that has been dropped into a paragraph.
- Identify a weak claim and revise it to be more specific.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which is a claim? Choices: The quote is on page 4.; The narrator is unreliable.; “I never said that.”; The story has ten paragraphs.
- **Correct Answer:** The narrator is unreliable.
- **Explanation:** It is an arguable interpretation needing support.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.CLAIM

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q02 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which evidence best supports “The narrator is nervous”? Choices: She taps her foot and whispers, “What if I fail?”; The room has a door.; It is Tuesday.; The table is wooden.
- **Correct Answer:** She taps her foot and whispers, “What if I fail?”
- **Explanation:** It directly shows nervous behavior and words.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.EVIDENCE

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q03 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Write reasoning for this evidence: “He returned the lost wallet.” Claim: He is honest.
- **Correct Answer:** Returning the wallet shows honesty because he chose to give back something that was not his.
- **Explanation:** Reasoning connects action to trait.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.REASONING

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q04 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student writes a quote and then stops. What is missing?
- **Correct Answer:** Reasoning/explanation of how the quote supports the claim.
- **Explanation:** Quote dropping misconception.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.QUOTE_DROP

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q05 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which evidence is least relevant to “The school should start later because teens need sleep”? Choices: Research shows teens need 8–10 hours sleep.; Later start schools report better attendance.; The school mascot is a tiger.; Students say they feel tired in first period.
- **Correct Answer:** The school mascot is a tiger.
- **Explanation:** It does not support the sleep claim.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.RELEVANCE

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q06 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why can reasoning be more than one sentence?
- **Correct Answer:** Complex evidence may need several sentences to explain meaning, context, and connection to the claim.
- **Explanation:** Deep explanation.
- **Skill Tag:** WRITING.ANALYSIS

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q07 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Create a claim about a character who helps others despite danger.
- **Correct Answer:** Example: The character is selfless/brave because they risk safety to help others.
- **Explanation:** Claim should be arguable and specific.
- **Skill Tag:** CREATE.CLAIM

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q08 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** What should a counterclaim do? Choices: Ignore the other side; Present an opposing idea fairly; Replace all evidence; End the essay immediately
- **Correct Answer:** Present an opposing idea fairly
- **Explanation:** Counterclaims show awareness of complexity.
- **Skill Tag:** ARGUMENT.COUNTERCLAIM

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q09 [evidence | hard]
- **Question:** Rank two pieces of evidence and explain which is stronger.
- **Correct Answer:** Answer should choose stronger, more direct evidence and justify relevance.
- **Explanation:** Evidence evaluation.
- **Skill Tag:** CRITICAL.RANK_EVIDENCE

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q10 [apply | hard]
- **Question:** Write a complete CER about this claim: “Practice leads to improvement.”
- **Correct Answer:** Claim plus specific evidence and reasoning.
- **Explanation:** Must contain all CER parts.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.PARAGRAPH

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q11 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** What does relevance mean? Choices: How long evidence is; How directly evidence connects to the claim; How old the source is only; How fancy the words sound
- **Correct Answer:** How directly evidence connects to the claim
- **Explanation:** Relevance is connection to claim.
- **Skill Tag:** VOCAB.RELEVANCE

### SA-G9-ENG-U3-L1-Q12 [reflection | medium]
- **Question:** After receiving feedback “your evidence is weak,” what should you check first?
- **Correct Answer:** Whether the evidence directly supports the claim and whether stronger proof exists.
- **Explanation:** Metacognitive revision.
- **Skill Tag:** METACOG.REVISE

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student thinks evidence alone proves the claim without reasoning.
- **Simple Explanation:** Evidence is the proof. Reasoning is the bridge that tells the reader how the proof supports the claim.

### Reteach Steps
1. Underline the claim.
2. Circle evidence words.
3. Ask “How does this prove it?”
4. Write reasoning using because/this shows.
5. Check whether the paragraph answers “so what?”

### Reteach Practice

- CER letters → **Claim, Evidence, Reasoning**
- Missing part after quote → **Reasoning**
- Relevant evidence for “tired”: “yawning” or “blue shoes”? → **Yawn**
- Fix: “He is kind. Quote.” → **Add explanation of how the quote shows kindness.**
- Counterclaim means? → **Opposing/alternative claim**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.
