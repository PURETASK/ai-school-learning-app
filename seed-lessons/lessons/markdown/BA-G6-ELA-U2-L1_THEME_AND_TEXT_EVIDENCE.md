## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will infer a theme from a literary passage and support it using relevant text evidence and reasoning.
- **Essential Question:** How do readers prove what a story teaches about life?
- **Development Notes:** Middle-school students often name a topic instead of a theme. Require sentence-level theme statements and evidence-based reasoning.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **theme:** a message or lesson about life in a story
- **topic:** a subject a story includes
- **inference:** a conclusion based on evidence and reasoning
- **text evidence:** words or events from the text that support an idea
- **reasoning:** the explanation that connects evidence to a claim

### Prerequisites
- Summarize plot events
- Identify character actions and conflicts
- Quote or paraphrase from a text

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

Students read a short scene where a character admits a mistake even though it is difficult. They decide whether the theme is “honesty,” “honesty requires courage,” or “the character lied.”

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will infer a theme from a literary passage and support it using relevant text evidence and reasoning.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

A theme is not just a topic like friendship or courage. A strong theme is a full statement about that topic, such as “Real courage means doing the right thing even when it is difficult.” Readers infer theme from character choices, conflict, consequences, and repeated ideas.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Topic: courage. Evidence: Maya tells the truth about breaking the model even though she fears punishment. Consequence: her group trusts her again. Theme: Courage can mean telling the truth when it is hard. Reasoning: Maya’s choice shows courage because she risks consequences to repair trust.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** Two pieces of evidence are offered. One shows a character being kind; one only describes the setting. Which better supports a theme about kindness? Why?

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: The story teaches that honesty can rebuild trust. Choose two events as evidence and explain the connection.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret how the ending changes the reader’s understanding of the character’s choice.

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students build a Claim → Evidence → Reasoning card stack for a short passage.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** Theme is built from patterns in story evidence.

### Smallest Parts
- topic
- character choices
- conflict
- consequences
- repeated ideas
- theme statement
- supporting evidence

### Known Facts
- Characters make choices.
- Stories show consequences.
- Repeated ideas often point to meaning.

### Unknowns
- The deeper message about life.

### Rules / Patterns
- A theme should be a full sentence.
- A theme should apply beyond one character or event.
- Evidence must come from the text.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not confuse topic with theme.
- Do not choose evidence that only summarizes plot without supporting the message.
- Do not overstate a theme the text does not support.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Identify a topic.
- Track character choices and consequences.
- Look for patterns.
- Write a theme statement.
- Choose evidence.
- Explain how the evidence supports the theme.

### Answer Check
- If the theme works only for one tiny event, revise it to be broader but still text-supported.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Sort statements into topic, plot detail, and theme.
- Choose the strongest evidence for a theme about perseverance.
- Rewrite the topic “friendship” as a theme sentence.
- Use because to connect evidence to reasoning.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which is a theme, not a topic? Choices: courage; friendship; Hard work can help people overcome obstacles.; the forest
- **Correct Answer:** Hard work can help people overcome obstacles.
- **Explanation:** It is a full message about life.
- **Skill Tag:** ELA.THEME

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q02 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which evidence best supports a theme about perseverance? Choices: The sky was gray.; Lena practiced every day after failing the first test.; The room had three windows.; Lena liked blue shoes.
- **Correct Answer:** Lena practiced every day after failing the first test.
- **Explanation:** It shows continuing despite difficulty.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.RELEVANCE

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q03 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Turn the topic “honesty” into a theme statement.
- **Correct Answer:** Example: Honesty can be difficult, but it helps rebuild trust.
- **Explanation:** Must be a full message about life.
- **Skill Tag:** CREATE.THEME

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q04 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student says the theme is “the dog ran away.” What is wrong?
- **Correct Answer:** That is a plot event, not a life message.
- **Explanation:** Theme is deeper than plot summary.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.PLOT_THEME

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q05 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why does a theme need text evidence?
- **Correct Answer:** Evidence proves the theme is supported by the story instead of guessed.
- **Explanation:** Claim must be justified.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.REASONING

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q06 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which detail would help infer theme most? Choices: main character choice; page number; font style; chapter title color
- **Correct Answer:** main character choice
- **Explanation:** Character choices reveal meaning.
- **Skill Tag:** INTERPRET.PATTERN

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q07 [cer | medium]
- **Question:** Claim: The story shows that trust must be earned. What evidence would support this claim?
- **Correct Answer:** Evidence where a character acts responsibly or repairs harm and others trust them again.
- **Explanation:** Assess relevance and reasoning.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.EVIDENCE

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q08 [discussion | medium]
- **Question:** Use the frame: “I interpret the theme as ___ because ___.”
- **Correct Answer:** Valid theme plus evidence-based reason.
- **Explanation:** Structured academic dialogue.
- **Skill Tag:** DISCUSSION.FRAME

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q09 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which is the weakest evidence for a theme about sacrifice? Choices: A character gives up free time to help someone.; A character shares food when hungry.; A character buys a red notebook.; A character takes a risk for a friend.
- **Correct Answer:** A character buys a red notebook.
- **Explanation:** It does not show sacrifice.
- **Skill Tag:** CRITICAL.EVIDENCE_QUALITY

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q10 [apply | medium]
- **Question:** A character fails twice but tries a new strategy and finally succeeds. Infer a theme.
- **Correct Answer:** Perseverance and learning from failure can lead to success.
- **Explanation:** Theme should generalize from the evidence.
- **Skill Tag:** INTERPRET.THEME

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q11 [short | easy]
- **Question:** What is the reasoning part of CER?
- **Correct Answer:** None
- **Explanation:** The explanation of how the evidence supports the claim.
- **Skill Tag:** CER.REASONING

### BA-G6-ELA-U2-L1-Q12 [challenge | hard]
- **Question:** Give a counter-theme someone might argue and explain what evidence would be needed.
- **Correct Answer:** A valid alternate theme plus necessary supporting evidence.
- **Explanation:** Advanced interpretation.
- **Skill Tag:** CRITICAL.COUNTERARGUMENT

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student names a topic or plot event instead of a theme statement.
- **Simple Explanation:** A topic is one word or idea. A theme is what the story says about that idea.

### Reteach Steps
1. Name a topic.
2. Find two character choices or consequences.
3. Ask what lesson those events suggest.
4. Write a full theme statement.
5. Attach evidence and reasoning.

### Reteach Practice

- Topic or theme: friendship → **Topic**
- Topic or theme: True friends tell each other the truth → **Theme**
- Best evidence: character apologizes or room is cold? → **Character apologizes**
- Fix: Theme = “the race” → **Trying again after failure can lead to growth.**
- CER: What comes after evidence? → **Reasoning**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.

## 14. Challenge Path
- Compare two possible themes and defend the stronger one.
- Write a CER paragraph using two pieces of evidence.
- Identify a counterargument and explain why your theme is better supported.
