## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will model the water cycle as a system, explain how water moves through reservoirs, and use evidence to describe the role of energy.
- **Essential Question:** How does water move through Earth’s systems without disappearing?
- **Development Notes:** Students may memorize stage names without understanding energy, matter movement, and system interactions. Emphasize system flow and cause/effect.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **evaporation:** liquid water changing into water vapor
- **condensation:** water vapor cooling into liquid droplets
- **precipitation:** water falling from clouds as rain, snow, sleet, or hail
- **collection:** water gathering in oceans, lakes, rivers, soil, ice, or groundwater
- **transpiration:** water vapor released by plants
- **reservoir:** a place where water is stored

### Prerequisites
- Know states of matter: solid, liquid, gas
- Understand basic weather terms
- Read arrows in diagrams

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

Students imagine a single water droplet traveling from the ocean to a cloud, to a mountain, into a river, and back to the ocean. They predict what forces move it.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will model the water cycle as a system, explain how water moves through reservoirs, and use evidence to describe the role of energy.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

The water cycle is a system powered mainly by the Sun and gravity. Water changes form and moves among reservoirs such as oceans, atmosphere, land, ice, living things, and groundwater. The amount of water is conserved; it changes location and form.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Ocean water gains energy from sunlight and evaporates into water vapor. The vapor rises and cools, condensing into cloud droplets. Droplets combine and fall as precipitation. Some water runs into rivers, some enters soil, and some returns to the ocean.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** Why is “water cycle” a simplified model instead of an exact path every water molecule follows?

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: The Sun helps move water through the cycle. Use evaporation as evidence and explain the energy transfer.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret arrows in a diagram: which arrows show energy-driven movement and which show gravity-driven movement?

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students simulate a water droplet journey by rolling process cards and building a path through reservoirs.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** The water cycle is movement of water through a connected Earth system.

### Smallest Parts
- water reservoirs
- state changes
- solar energy
- gravity
- atmosphere
- landforms
- living things

### Known Facts
- The Sun adds energy that can evaporate water.
- Cooling can cause condensation.
- Gravity pulls precipitation and runoff downhill.
- Water can be stored in many reservoirs.

### Unknowns
- The path a specific water droplet will take next.

### Rules / Patterns
- Matter is conserved in the system.
- Energy drives state changes.
- Gravity drives downward movement.
- Systems contain connected parts.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not think water disappears when it evaporates.
- Do not treat the cycle as always following one perfect circle.
- Do not ignore groundwater, ice, plants, or storage.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Identify the current reservoir.
- Identify the process moving water.
- Name the energy or force involved.
- Track the next reservoir.
- Explain how the parts connect.

### Answer Check
- If water seems to disappear, ask which form or reservoir it moved into.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Label processes on a water cycle diagram.
- Explain which process requires heat energy.
- Sort examples into reservoirs: atmosphere, ocean, groundwater, ice, living things.
- Debug a diagram that skips collection and groundwater.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which process changes liquid water into water vapor? Choices: condensation; precipitation; evaporation; collection
- **Correct Answer:** evaporation
- **Explanation:** Evaporation changes liquid to gas.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.WATER_CYCLE.VOCAB

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q02 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which process forms clouds? Choices: condensation; runoff; collection; melting
- **Correct Answer:** condensation
- **Explanation:** Water vapor cools into droplets.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.WATER_CYCLE.PROCESS

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q03 [short | easy]
- **Question:** Name two water reservoirs.
- **Correct Answer:** Examples: ocean, lake, river, atmosphere, ice, groundwater, soil, plants.
- **Explanation:** Reservoirs store water.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.SYSTEMS.RESERVOIR

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q04 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why is the Sun important to the water cycle?
- **Correct Answer:** It provides energy for evaporation and helps drive movement of water into the atmosphere.
- **Explanation:** Energy reasoning required.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.ENERGY

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q05 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student says evaporated water is gone forever. Find and fix the error.
- **Correct Answer:** It is not gone; it changed into water vapor in the atmosphere.
- **Explanation:** Matter changes form/location.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.MATTER_CONSERVATION

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q06 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** What force causes rainwater to flow downhill into rivers? Choices: magnetism; gravity; sound; moonlight
- **Correct Answer:** gravity
- **Explanation:** Gravity pulls water downward.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.FORCES.GRAVITY

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q07 [apply | medium]
- **Question:** A puddle shrinks on a hot day. Which process is likely happening?
- **Correct Answer:** Evaporation.
- **Explanation:** Heat energy causes liquid water to become vapor.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.APPLY

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q08 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which is NOT always part of a simple water cycle diagram but is part of the real system? Choices: groundwater; clouds; rain; evaporation
- **Correct Answer:** groundwater
- **Explanation:** Groundwater is often omitted in simple diagrams but is important.
- **Skill Tag:** SYSTEMS.COMPLEXITY

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q09 [short | medium]
- **Question:** What is transpiration?
- **Correct Answer:** Water vapor released by plants.
- **Explanation:** Plants are part of the water cycle.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.VOCAB

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q10 [evidence | hard]
- **Question:** Use evidence to support: “The water cycle is a system.”
- **Correct Answer:** It has connected parts such as reservoirs, processes, energy, and gravity that affect one another.
- **Explanation:** Must name connected parts.
- **Skill Tag:** SYSTEMS.EVIDENCE

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q11 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which sequence is reasonable? Choices: evaporation → condensation → precipitation; precipitation → evaporation → ocean disappears; condensation → gravity → sunlight; collection → no movement → evaporation impossible
- **Correct Answer:** evaporation → condensation → precipitation
- **Explanation:** This follows common water-cycle processes.
- **Skill Tag:** SCI.SEQUENCE

### BA-G6-SCI-U2-L1-Q12 [challenge | hard]
- **Question:** Describe a droplet path that includes groundwater before returning to the ocean.
- **Correct Answer:** Example: precipitation infiltrates soil, enters groundwater, flows to stream, returns to ocean.
- **Explanation:** Complex systems path.
- **Skill Tag:** SYSTEMS.MODEL

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student memorizes a simple circle and thinks water disappears or follows only one fixed path.
- **Simple Explanation:** Water changes form and moves through connected places. The path can vary, but matter remains in the system.

### Reteach Steps
1. Define reservoir.
2. Trace one droplet path.
3. Label the process at each movement.
4. Name the energy or force involved.
5. Add an overlooked reservoir like groundwater.

### Reteach Practice

- Liquid to vapor = ? → **Evaporation**
- Vapor to droplets = ? → **Condensation**
- Water falling from clouds = ? → **Precipitation**
- Force pulling runoff downhill → **Gravity**
- Fix: evaporated water disappears → **It becomes vapor in the atmosphere.**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.
