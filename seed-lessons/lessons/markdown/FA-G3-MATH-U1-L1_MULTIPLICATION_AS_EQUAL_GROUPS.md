## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will explain multiplication as equal groups, represent multiplication with repeated addition and arrays, and solve one-step equal-group word problems.
- **Essential Question:** What does multiplication really mean before we calculate it?
- **Development Notes:** Third graders are moving from repeated addition into multiplicative reasoning. Keep models concrete and visual before asking for equations.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **equal groups:** groups that each have the same amount
- **factor:** a number being multiplied
- **product:** the answer to a multiplication problem
- **array:** objects arranged in equal rows and columns
- **repeated addition:** adding the same number again and again

### Prerequisites
- Count by 2s, 5s, and 10s
- Add within 100
- Recognize rows, columns, and groups of objects

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

A garden has 4 baskets. Each basket has 5 apples. The gardener writes 4 × 5. Students predict what each number means before finding the total.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will explain multiplication as equal groups, represent multiplication with repeated addition and arrays, and solve one-step equal-group word problems.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

Multiplication is a way to count equal groups efficiently. In 4 × 5, there are 4 groups and each group has 5. That can be written as 5 + 5 + 5 + 5, drawn as 4 rows of 5, or solved as 20.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Problem: 3 boxes have 6 crayons in each box. Known facts: 3 boxes, 6 crayons each. Repeated addition: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18. Multiplication: 3 × 6 = 18. Answer: 18 crayons.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** A student says 4 × 5 means 4 + 5. What is the mistake? Explain how equal groups help fix the reasoning.

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: 3 × 4 = 12. Prove the claim using at least two kinds of evidence: repeated addition, an array, or a word story.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** An array with 2 rows and 6 columns can be read as 2 groups of 6 or 6 columns of 2. Discuss what changes and what stays the same.

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students drag objects into equal baskets. The app highlights unequal groups and asks students to debug the model before writing the equation.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** Multiplication describes equal groups.

### Smallest Parts
- number of groups
- amount in each group
- total amount
- operation symbol
- model or story context

### Known Facts
- There are 4 baskets.
- Each basket has 5 apples.
- All baskets have the same number of apples.

### Unknowns
- Total number of apples.

### Rules / Patterns
- Equal groups can be counted with repeated addition.
- A multiplication expression can represent repeated equal groups.
- If the groups are not equal, multiplication does not directly fit until the groups are adjusted or described differently.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not add the two factors once, such as 4 + 5.
- Do not multiply when groups are unequal without explaining the change.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Name the groups.
- Name the amount in each group.
- Write repeated addition.
- Convert repeated addition to multiplication.
- Solve and check by skip-counting or drawing.

### Answer Check
- Skip-count by the amount in each group: 5, 10, 15, 20.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Draw 2 groups of 4 stars. Write the repeated addition and multiplication expression.
- Match 5 + 5 + 5 to 3 × 5. Explain why it is not 5 × 3 in this story model.
- Use an array to show 4 × 3 and label rows and columns.
- Debug this model: 3 groups are shown as 4, 4, and 5. Can it be represented by one multiplication fact? Why or why not?

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student adds the two factors instead of forming equal groups.
- **Simple Explanation:** Multiplication asks two questions: how many groups, and how many in each group?

### Reteach Steps
1. Circle each group.
2. Write the same addend once for each group.
3. Convert repeated addition to multiplication.
4. Solve with skip-counting.
5. Check with a drawing or array.

### Reteach Practice

- 2 groups of 3 = ? → **6**
- 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 as multiplication → **4 × 3**
- 4 groups of 2 = ? → **8**
- Which is equal groups: 2,2,2 or 2,3,2? → **2,2,2**
- Fix: 5 × 3 = 8 → **5 × 3 = 15**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.

## 14. Challenge Path
- Create two different arrays that both show 12. Explain why both work.
- Write a word problem for 6 × 4 and solve it using repeated addition, an array, and multiplication.
- Compare 3 × 4 and 4 × 3. Explain what changes in the story and what stays the same.
