# FA-G3-WR-U1-L1 — Building a Strong Paragraph

**Academy:** Foundation Academy  
**Grade:** 3  
**Subject/Course:** Writing / Grade 3 Writing  
**Unit:** Unit 1: Paragraph Foundations  
**Estimated Time:** 25 minutes

## Learning Objective
Students will write a focused paragraph with a topic sentence, supporting details, and a closing sentence.

## Essential Question
How do writers build a paragraph that makes sense to a reader?

## Standards Tags
- WR.G3.PARAGRAPH.STRUCTURE
- ELA.G3.EVIDENCE.SUPPORT

## Thinking Skill Tags
- evidence-based reasoning
- critical thinking
- metacognition

## Vocabulary
- **topic sentence**: The sentence that tells the main idea.
- **supporting detail**: A sentence that explains or proves the main idea.
- **closing sentence**: A sentence that wraps up the paragraph.
- **revise**: To improve writing.

## Universal Lesson Flow

### Hook
A messy paragraph has good ideas but no clear main idea, weak details, and missing closing sentence. The student becomes a paragraph builder.

**Student Action:** Notice what feels confusing and name what the paragraph needs.

**Success Criteria:**
- Identifies that paragraphs need one main idea and supporting details.

### Learning Goal
I can write a strong paragraph with a topic sentence, supporting details, and a closing sentence.

**Student Action:** Read the goal aloud and predict the three paragraph parts.

**Success Criteria:**
- Names topic sentence, details, and closing sentence.

### Mini Teach
A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. The topic sentence tells the idea, details prove or explain it, and the closing sentence wraps it up.

**Student Action:** Match each paragraph part to its job.

**Success Criteria:**
- Correctly matches all three paragraph parts.

### First-Principles Breakdown
Break writing down: What is my one idea? What facts or examples support it? What sentence helps the reader feel finished?

**Student Action:** Fill in one idea, three supports, and one ending.

**Success Criteria:**
- Separates idea from evidence/details.

### Worked Example
Topic: Dogs can be helpful pets. Details: they warn families, guide people, and give companionship. Closing: That is why dogs can be more than just pets.

**Student Action:** Point to the topic sentence, details, and closing sentence.

**Success Criteria:**
- Labels each sentence function correctly.

### Guided Practice
Build a paragraph about a favorite school subject using sentence frames.

**Student Action:** Complete frames: My topic is ___. One reason is ___. Another detail is ___. This shows ___.

**Success Criteria:**
- Creates a coherent paragraph using frames.

### Critical Thinking Checkpoint
Which detail best supports the topic sentence: 'Reading helps people learn new things'?

**Student Action:** Compare strong, weak, and unrelated details.

**Success Criteria:**
- Explains why one detail is strongest.

### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task
Use Answer → Proof → Explanation: Answer what your paragraph is about, choose details that prove it, explain how they fit.

**Student Action:** Sort details into strong support, weak support, and not related.

**Success Criteria:**
- Chooses details that directly support the topic.

### Interpretation / Discussion Task
Discussion frame: 'I think this detail belongs because ___. I would remove ___ because ___.'

**Student Action:** Use sentence frames to discuss paragraph choices.

**Success Criteria:**
- Explains a revision choice respectfully.

### Active Practice
Write a five-sentence paragraph about why playgrounds are important.

**Student Action:** Draft topic sentence, three details, and closing sentence.

**Success Criteria:**
- Includes all paragraph parts.

### Retrieval Check
Without looking, name the three main parts of a strong paragraph.

**Student Action:** Recall topic sentence, details, closing sentence.

**Success Criteria:**
- Recalls all three parts.

### Feedback
Feedback checks structure first, then evidence quality, then sentence clarity.

**Student Action:** Revise one sentence based on feedback.

**Success Criteria:**
- Improves the paragraph after feedback.

### Reflection
What part of the paragraph was easiest? What part needs more practice?

**Student Action:** Choose one writing goal for next time.

**Success Criteria:**
- Names one specific writing improvement target.

## Quiz and Answer Key

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q1** (mc): What is the job of a topic sentence?  
  - Correct/Expected: To tell the paragraph's main idea  
  - Explanation: The topic sentence tells the reader what the paragraph is mostly about.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q2** (mc): Which detail best supports 'Plants need care to grow'?  
  - Correct/Expected: Water and sunlight help plants stay healthy  
  - Explanation: This detail directly supports the claim about plant care.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q3** (mc): What should a closing sentence do?  
  - Correct/Expected: Wrap up the paragraph  
  - Explanation: A closing sentence gives the paragraph a finished feeling.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q4** (mc): Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph about healthy snacks?  
  - Correct/Expected: A video game can be exciting  
  - Explanation: That detail is unrelated to healthy snacks.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q5** (mc): Which order is strongest?  
  - Correct/Expected: Topic, details, closing  
  - Explanation: A clear paragraph starts with the main idea, supports it, then wraps up.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q6** (mc): A paragraph should mostly be about how many main ideas?  
  - Correct/Expected: One  
  - Explanation: A focused paragraph develops one main idea.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q7** (short_answer): Write a topic sentence for a paragraph about why books are useful.  
  - Correct/Expected: Books are useful because they help people learn, imagine, and understand new ideas.  
  - Explanation: A strong topic sentence states the main idea clearly.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q8** (explain): Explain why details must match the topic sentence.  
  - Correct/Expected: Details must match because they prove or explain the main idea for the reader.  
  - Explanation: Details are evidence for the topic; unrelated details confuse the reader.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q9** (cer): Use Claim → Evidence → Reasoning for this claim: Parks help communities.  
  - Correct/Expected: Claim: Parks help communities. Evidence: People can play, exercise, and meet safely. Reasoning: These activities help people connect and stay healthy.  
  - Explanation: The answer should include a claim, evidence, and reasoning.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q10** (short_answer): Revise this weak closing: 'That is it.'  
  - Correct/Expected: That is why playgrounds are important places for kids and families.  
  - Explanation: A stronger closing connects back to the paragraph's main idea.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q11** (explain): Explain one important idea from building a strong paragraph using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains building a strong paragraph and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain building a strong paragraph, not just recognize it.

- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-Q12** (explain): Explain one important idea from building a strong paragraph using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains building a strong paragraph and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain building a strong paragraph, not just recognize it.

## Memory Vault Items
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV1**: What are the three main parts of a strong paragraph? → Topic sentence, supporting details, and closing sentence.
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV2**: What does topic sentence mean? → A sentence that tells the main idea of a paragraph.
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV3**: Choose the stronger detail for 'Exercise is healthy': 'Running strengthens the body' or 'Shoes have laces.' → Running strengthens the body.
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV4**: Why should details match the topic? → Because details explain or prove the main idea.
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV5**: What does a closing sentence do? → It wraps up the paragraph and connects back to the main idea.
- **FA-G3-WR-U1-L1-MV6**: What should you do if a detail is interesting but unrelated? → Remove it or save it for a different paragraph.

## Reteach Path
A paragraph is like a small team. Every sentence must help the same main idea.

- Choose one topic
- Write one topic sentence
- Add three matching details
- Add a closing sentence
- Remove details that do not belong

## Challenge Path
- Write a paragraph with a topic sentence, three details, and one example of evidence.
- Revise a partner-style sample paragraph by removing weak details.
- Turn the paragraph into a short oral explanation for a parent.
