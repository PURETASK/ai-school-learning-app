## 2. Learning Objective and Essential Question

- **Learning Objective:** Students will describe ratios as relationships between quantities, write ratios in multiple forms, and use ratio reasoning to solve contextual problems.
- **Essential Question:** How can one relationship describe two quantities at the same time?
- **Development Notes:** Grade 6 students often treat ratios as subtraction or isolated fractions. Emphasize relationship between two quantities and multiple equivalent representations.

## 4. Vocabulary and Prerequisites

### Vocabulary
- **ratio:** a comparison of two quantities
- **equivalent ratio:** a ratio that describes the same relationship using different numbers
- **part-to-part:** a comparison between two parts
- **part-to-whole:** a comparison between one part and the total
- **rate:** a ratio comparing quantities with different units

### Prerequisites
- Understand multiplication and division facts
- Read simple fractions
- Compare quantities using words and numbers

## 6. Lesson Flow


### Hook (3–5 min)

A trail mix has 2 cups of raisins for every 3 cups of nuts. Students decide whether a batch with 4 cups raisins and 6 cups nuts tastes the same, stronger, or weaker.

**Student Action:** Predict, notice, or explain initial thinking before direct instruction.

**Teacher/App Action:** Capture initial response as non-graded diagnostic evidence.


### Learning Goal (1 min)

I can students will describe ratios as relationships between quantities, write ratios in multiple forms, and use ratio reasoning to solve contextual problems.


### Mini Teach (6–8 min)

A ratio compares quantities. The order matters because 2 raisins to 3 nuts is not the same statement as 3 raisins to 2 nuts. Ratios can be written as 2 to 3, 2:3, or 2/3 when comparing raisins to nuts. Equivalent ratios keep the same relationship.


### Worked Example (5–7 min)

Problem: There are 6 red tiles and 9 blue tiles. Ratio red to blue is 6:9. Divide both by 3 to simplify to 2:3. Equivalent examples include 4:6 and 8:12.

**Teacher/App Action:** Highlight each reasoning step and connect it to the first-principles breakdown.


### Critical Thinking Checkpoint (3–5 min)

**Prompt:** Why is adding the same number to both parts not a reliable way to create equivalent ratios?

**Look For:** Student identifies assumption, evaluates reasoning, and explains why one answer or model is stronger.


### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Claim: 6:9 is equivalent to 2:3. Use division or a ratio table as evidence.

**Required Structure:** Claim → Evidence → Reasoning


### Interpretation / Discussion Task (5–7 min)

**Prompt:** Interpret a double number line showing cups of raisins and nuts. What does each matched pair mean?

**Safety:** Use private response or moderated classroom mode only. No unrestricted student chat.


### Active Practice (8–10 min)

Students complete a ratio table for trail mix and test which batches preserve the same taste.

**Data Captured:**
- completion
- attempts
- hint usage
- mistake type
- confidence


### Reflection (2–3 min)

**Prompt:** What strategy helped you most, what mistake should you watch for, and when will you review this skill again?


## 7. First-Principles Breakdown

- **Core Concept:** A ratio is a relationship between quantities.

### Smallest Parts
- first quantity
- second quantity
- order of comparison
- units/context
- multiplicative relationship

### Known Facts
- The mix has 2 cups raisins for every 3 cups nuts.
- Both quantities can scale together.
- The relationship matters more than the raw size of the batch.

### Unknowns
- Whether another pair of quantities preserves the same relationship.

### Rules / Patterns
- Multiply or divide both quantities by the same factor to make an equivalent ratio.
- Ratio order must match the meaning.
- Part-to-part and part-to-whole comparisons answer different questions.

### Assumptions to Avoid
- Do not subtract quantities to compare ratio relationships.
- Do not reverse the ratio without changing the meaning.
- Do not ignore units.

### Step-by-Step Rebuild
- Identify the two quantities.
- Decide order and comparison type.
- Write the ratio.
- Scale both parts by the same factor.
- Check whether the relationship stayed constant.

### Answer Check
- For 2:3 and 4:6, both parts doubled, so the relationship stayed the same.

## 8. Guided Practice
- Write three forms of the ratio 5 dogs to 2 cats.
- Identify whether 3:4 is part-to-part or part-to-whole in a class with 3 sixth graders and 4 seventh graders.
- Scale 2:5 by 3 and explain the result.
- Debug: A student says 2:3 and 4:5 are equivalent because both increased by 2.

## 10. Quiz and Answer Key

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q01 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** Which ratio means 5 apples to 8 oranges? Choices: 8:5; 5:8; 5+8; 8-5
- **Correct Answer:** 5:8
- **Explanation:** The order is apples first, oranges second.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.RATIO_LANGUAGE

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q02 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Scale the ratio 2:7 by 4.
- **Correct Answer:** 8:28
- **Explanation:** Multiply both parts by 4.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.EQUIVALENT_RATIO

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q03 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Which is equivalent to 3:5? Choices: 6:10; 4:6; 9:10; 5:3
- **Correct Answer:** 6:10
- **Explanation:** Both parts were multiplied by 2.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.EQUIVALENT_RATIO

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q04 [error | medium]
- **Question:** A student says 2:3 is equivalent to 4:5 because both numbers increased by 2. Find the error.
- **Correct Answer:** Equivalent ratios require multiplying/dividing both parts by the same factor, not adding the same number.
- **Explanation:** Targets additive reasoning.
- **Skill Tag:** MISTAKE.ADDITIVE_RATIO

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q05 [explain | medium]
- **Question:** Why does 3:2 not mean the same as 2:3?
- **Correct Answer:** The order reverses which quantity is first and changes the relationship in context.
- **Explanation:** Order carries meaning.
- **Skill Tag:** CRITICAL.ORDER

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q06 [apply | medium]
- **Question:** A recipe uses 1 cup rice for every 2 cups water. How much water for 4 cups rice?
- **Correct Answer:** 8 cups water
- **Explanation:** Scale 1:2 by 4.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.RATIO_CONTEXT

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q07 [mc | easy]
- **Question:** A class has 12 students: 5 play soccer and 7 do not. What is the ratio soccer to not soccer? Choices: 5:7; 7:5; 5:12; 12:5
- **Correct Answer:** 5:7
- **Explanation:** It asks part-to-part.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.PART_TO_PART

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q08 [mc | medium]
- **Question:** Using the same class, what is the ratio soccer to total students? Choices: 5:7; 7:12; 5:12; 12:7
- **Correct Answer:** 5:12
- **Explanation:** It asks part-to-whole.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.PART_TO_WHOLE

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q09 [short | medium]
- **Question:** Simplify 10:15.
- **Correct Answer:** 2:3
- **Explanation:** Divide both parts by 5.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.SIMPLIFY

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q10 [evidence | medium]
- **Question:** Prove that 12:18 and 2:3 are equivalent.
- **Correct Answer:** Divide both 12 and 18 by 6, or multiply 2:3 by 6.
- **Explanation:** Requires evidence.
- **Skill Tag:** EVIDENCE.RATIO

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q11 [mc | hard]
- **Question:** Which pair is NOT equivalent to 4:6? Choices: 2:3; 8:12; 12:18; 6:8
- **Correct Answer:** 6:8
- **Explanation:** 6:8 simplifies to 3:4, not 2:3.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.DISTRACTOR

### BA-G6-MATH-U1-L1-Q12 [apply | hard]
- **Question:** Design a sports drink recipe with a ratio of 2 scoops powder to 5 cups water for 20 cups water.
- **Correct Answer:** 8 scoops powder
- **Explanation:** Scale 5 cups water to 20 by multiplying by 4, so 2 scoops becomes 8.
- **Skill Tag:** MATH.MULTISTEP

## 13. Reteach Path

- **Misconception:** Student uses additive comparison instead of multiplicative ratio reasoning.
- **Simple Explanation:** Equivalent ratios keep the same relationship by multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number.

### Reteach Steps
1. Label the two quantities.
2. Write the original ratio.
3. Choose a scale factor.
4. Multiply both parts by the same factor.
5. Check with a table or double number line.

### Reteach Practice

- Scale 1:3 by 2 → **2:6**
- Simplify 6:12 → **1:2**
- Equivalent to 2:5: 4:10 or 4:7? → **4:10**
- Fix: 3:4 → 5:6 by adding 2 → **Not equivalent; scale by multiplication instead.**
- Part-to-whole in 2 red and 3 blue: red to total → **2:5**

- **Exit Criteria:** Student completes at least 4 of 5 reteach items and explains the corrected misconception.
