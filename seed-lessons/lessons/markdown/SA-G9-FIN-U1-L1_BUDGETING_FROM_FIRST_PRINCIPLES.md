# SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1 — Budgeting from First Principles

**Academy:** Scholar Academy  
**Grade:** 9  
**Subject/Course:** Financial Literacy / Grade 9 Financial Literacy  
**Unit:** Unit 1: Money Decisions and Tradeoffs  
**Estimated Time:** 35 minutes

## Learning Objective
Students will create a simple budget by reasoning from income, needs, wants, savings, and tradeoffs.

## Essential Question
How can first-principles thinking help people make better money decisions?

## Standards Tags
- FIN.G9.BUDGETING.FOUNDATIONS
- MATH.G9.REASONING.NUMERICAL_MODELING

## Thinking Skill Tags
- first-principles problem solving
- critical thinking
- evidence-based reasoning
- life skills

## Vocabulary
- **income**: Money coming in.
- **budget**: A plan for how money will be used.
- **need**: Something required for basic responsibility, safety, or health.
- **want**: Something optional.
- **tradeoff**: Giving up one option to choose another.

## Universal Lesson Flow

### Hook
You have $100 for the week. Food, transportation, savings, and entertainment all compete for the same money.

**Student Action:** Decide what must be paid first and explain why.

**Success Criteria:**
- Distinguishes needs from wants.

### Learning Goal
I can build a simple budget from first principles by identifying income, needs, wants, savings, and tradeoffs.

**Student Action:** Restate budget as a plan for limited money.

**Success Criteria:**
- Defines budget and tradeoff.

### Mini Teach
A budget starts with income. Then subtract needs, choose savings, and decide how much is left for wants. Every choice has an opportunity cost.

**Student Action:** Classify expenses as needs, wants, or savings.

**Success Criteria:**
- Correctly classifies basic expenses.

### First-Principles Breakdown
Break budgeting down: How much money comes in? What must be paid? What future goal matters? What choices remain?

**Student Action:** Fill in income, fixed needs, variable choices, savings goal.

**Success Criteria:**
- Uses income minus expenses logic.

### Worked Example
Income $100. Needs: bus $20, lunch $30. Savings $10. Wants budget: $40. If entertainment costs $50, something must change.

**Student Action:** Calculate remaining money and identify the tradeoff.

**Success Criteria:**
- Calculates remaining budget accurately.

### Guided Practice
Build a $75 weekend budget with required transportation and food costs.

**Student Action:** Subtract needs, set savings, choose wants.

**Success Criteria:**
- Creates a balanced budget that does not overspend.

### Critical Thinking Checkpoint
Is a cheap option always the best option? Consider quality, safety, time, and goals.

**Student Action:** Compare two spending choices using constraints.

**Success Criteria:**
- Identifies at least one tradeoff beyond price.

### Evidence-Based Reasoning Task
Claim: This budget works. Evidence: total expenses are less than or equal to income. Reasoning: A budget cannot spend more than it has.

**Student Action:** Use numbers as evidence to defend a budget.

**Success Criteria:**
- Supports claim with calculation evidence.

### Interpretation / Discussion Task
Discussion frame: 'I would prioritize ___ because ___. The tradeoff is ___.'

**Student Action:** Explain a budget priority respectfully.

**Success Criteria:**
- Names a priority and tradeoff.

### Active Practice
Create a first-principles budget for a fictional student earning $120.

**Student Action:** Classify, calculate, adjust, and justify.

**Success Criteria:**
- Budget balances and includes a savings choice.

### Retrieval Check
Without looking, name the four budget building blocks.

**Student Action:** Recall income, needs, wants, savings/tradeoffs.

**Success Criteria:**
- Recalls core budget parts.

### Feedback
Feedback checks arithmetic, category choices, and reasoning about tradeoffs.

**Student Action:** Fix one overspending budget.

**Success Criteria:**
- Corrects the budget and explains the tradeoff.

### Reflection
What spending choice would be hardest for you to control, and what rule could help?

**Student Action:** Write one personal budgeting rule without sharing private financial data.

**Success Criteria:**
- Creates a general rule without sensitive personal disclosure.

## Quiz and Answer Key

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q1** (mc): What is income?  
  - Correct/Expected: Money coming in  
  - Explanation: A budget starts with money available.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q2** (mc): Which is usually a need?  
  - Correct/Expected: Bus fare to school  
  - Explanation: Needs are expenses required for basic responsibilities or safety.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q3** (mc): If income is $100 and needs cost $60, how much remains before savings or wants?  
  - Correct/Expected: $40  
  - Explanation: 100 - 60 = 40.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q4** (mc): What is a tradeoff?  
  - Correct/Expected: Giving up one option to choose another  
  - Explanation: Limited resources force choices.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q5** (mc): A budget spends $125 with income of $100. What is wrong?  
  - Correct/Expected: It overspends by $25  
  - Explanation: A balanced budget cannot spend more than income.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q6** (mc): Which evidence best proves a budget works?  
  - Correct/Expected: Total expenses are less than or equal to income  
  - Explanation: Numbers provide evidence that the budget balances.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q7** (short_answer): Build a budget: income $80, needs $50, savings $10. How much is left for wants?  
  - Correct/Expected: $20 is left for wants.  
  - Explanation: 80 - 50 - 10 = 20.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q8** (explain): Explain why savings can be treated like a planned category.  
  - Correct/Expected: Savings supports a future goal, so it should be planned before spending all remaining money on wants.  
  - Explanation: Savings becomes stronger when it is intentional.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q9** (cer): Use CER to defend a balanced budget.  
  - Correct/Expected: Claim: The budget works. Evidence: Expenses add to the same amount or less than income. Reasoning: The plan does not overspend.  
  - Explanation: Budget reasoning should use numerical evidence.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q10** (short_answer): Name one tradeoff in choosing a cheaper option.  
  - Correct/Expected: A cheaper option may save money but could take more time or be lower quality.  
  - Explanation: Tradeoffs include more than price.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q11** (explain): Explain one important idea from budgeting from first principles using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains budgeting from first principles and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain budgeting from first principles, not just recognize it.

- **SA-G9-FIN-U1-L1-Q12** (explain): Explain one important idea from budgeting from first principles using your own words.  
  - Correct/Expected: A strong answer accurately explains budgeting from first principles and includes a reason or example.  
  - Explanation: This checks whether the student can explain budgeting from first principles, not just recognize it.

## Reteach Path
A budget is math plus priorities. Start with income, subtract needs, choose savings, then decide wants.

- Write income
- List needs
- Subtract needs
- Choose savings
- Limit wants to what remains
- Check total

## Challenge Path
- Design two budgets with different priorities and compare the tradeoffs.
- Create a savings plan for a fictional $200 goal.
- Analyze an overspending budget and propose three fixes.
