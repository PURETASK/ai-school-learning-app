# Variables and Expressions

## Metadata

- Lesson ID: `SA-G9-ALG-U1-L1`
- Course: Algebra I
- Lesson Title: Variables and Expressions
- Estimated Time: 20–35 minutes depending on academy
- Learning Objective: Students interpret variables and expressions as tools for representing unknown quantities and relationships.
- Essential Question: Why do variables help us describe unknowns?
- Standards Tag: `ALG1.EXPRESSIONS.VARIABLES`
- Thinking Skill Tags: first-principles problem solving, abstraction, critical thinking
- Vocabulary: Variable; expression; coefficient; term; evaluate

## 1. Hook

Present a short scenario, image, puzzle, source, or problem that makes the concept necessary.

## 2. Learning Goal

Use an “I can” statement:

> I can explain variables and expressions and use evidence or reasoning to show my understanding.

## 3. Mini Teach

Provide a concise explanation with one visual or model. Use grade-appropriate language and avoid unnecessary vocabulary overload.

## 4. First-Principles Breakdown

Students identify:

- What is the concept/problem?
- What are the smallest parts?
- What do we know for sure?
- What is unknown or being asked?
- What rule, pattern, system, or relationship matters?
- What assumption should we avoid?
- How can we rebuild the answer step by step?

## 5. Worked Example

Show one fully solved example with reasoning exposed. Label the thinking moves, not just the answer.

## 6. Guided Practice

Students complete 2–4 scaffolded tasks with hints.

## 7. Critical Thinking Checkpoint

Prompt:

> What mistake might someone make here, and how would you catch it?

## 8. Evidence-Based Reasoning Task

Students must make a claim or answer and support it with evidence, data, text, model, or reasoning.

## 9. Interpretation / Discussion Task

Students interpret a passage, model, graph, situation, source, or explanation. If discussion is enabled, use sentence frames:

- I think ___ because ___.
- The evidence shows ___.
- Another possibility is ___.

## 10. Active Practice

Students complete an interactive task: sort, match, model, explain, solve, map, or debug.

## 11. Retrieval Check

Students answer 3–5 questions without looking back.

## 12. Feedback

Feedback must explain:

- what was correct/incorrect
- why
- what strategy to use next
- whether to reteach, review, or challenge

## 13. Mastery Score

Use mastery bands:

- 0–39 Needs Intervention
- 40–64 Needs Reteach
- 65–79 Almost Mastered
- 80–89 Mastered
- 90–100 Advanced

## 14. Spaced Review Scheduling

Create Memory Vault items for:

1. vocabulary/concept recall
2. explain-it-back prompt
3. application prompt
4. mistake correction prompt
5. cumulative review item

## 15. Reflection

Prompt:

> What was the most important idea, and what will you remember next time?

## 16. Reteach Path

Provide a simpler explanation, visual model, worked example, and short retry set.

## 17. Challenge Path

Provide a harder or more open-ended application task.

## Parent/Teacher Notes

Explain what the student learned, what weak skills to watch for, and how to support without giving the answer.

## Accessibility Notes

Use clear language, alt text, keyboard-accessible interactions, readable contrast, and no color-only meaning.
